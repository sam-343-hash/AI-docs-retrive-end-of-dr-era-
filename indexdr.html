How a search turns into a ranked result

This demo uses term-overlap scoring so the mechanics stay visible. A production system swaps step 2 and 3 for vector embeddings and a vector database, but the shape of the pipeline is the same.

01
Question in
The clinician's question is parsed into its clinically meaningful terms.
02
Match against corpus
Each source's title, tags, and body are scored against those terms.
03
Rank by relevance
Sources are ordered by match strength, not publish date or popularity.
04
Cite, don't summarize
The original passage is shown with matched terms highlighted, so the clinician reads the source itself.
Demonstration only. This tool runs on a small fixed sample corpus and simple keyword matching — it is not connected to a real medical database, is not validated for clinical use, and must not inform patient care. A production system would need a licensed clinical corpus, a vetted retrieval model, and review by clinical and regulatory teams before any real-world use.
